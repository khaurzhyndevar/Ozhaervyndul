# Ozhaervyndul

> A silver orchard grows where the serpent of dusk has hidden its last name.

## The Trees Behind the Curtain

The orchard is visible through a curtain hanging in an empty field. Walking around the curtain reveals only the other side of the field. Looking through it reveals silver trees beneath a low violet sky.

At noon, a woman brings a stool and sits beside the cloth. She repairs its lower edge with thread pulled from a glove. The glove has no opening for a hand. Its fingers move slightly whenever the orchard receives wind.

Travelers ask whether they may enter. She asks what they intend to leave outside. Most answer with an object. Some answer with a name. Those who answer with a season are asked to wait until their shadows reach the stool.

The woman never looks at those shadows. She measures them by the sound of fruit falling beyond the curtain.

## Fruit With No Interior

The silver trees bear dark pears. Cutting a pear reveals another unbroken skin beneath the first. Cutting again reveals a third. Those who continue eventually find their knife lying on the grass with no pear around it.

The knife then reflects a face that belongs to neither its owner nor any person standing nearby. This face is generally asleep. If it appears awake, the woman covers the blade with her glove and sits without moving until the violet sky becomes pale.

One traveler carried a pear out of the orchard. In the field, it became a little bell. In his pocket, it became a stone. At home, it became the handle of a door that had always opened without one.

He returned the following year carrying the entire door. The woman accepted it and placed it flat beneath the stool. From then on, her repairs cast a rectangular shadow on the grass.

## The Well Made of Branches

Within the orchard stands a well woven from living branches. Its water reflects clouds that do not cross the violet sky. Its bucket is a closed basket lined with blue feathers.

The woman lowers the basket once each afternoon. It returns holding a small quantity of darkness. She pours this around the youngest tree, whose trunk is no thicker than a wrist and whose roots enter the air instead of the soil.

The traveler with the door watched her do this. As the darkness touched the roots, he heard a familiar room being swept. He remembered the door's former place in his house, the uneven floor beyond it, and a cupboard he had never opened.

The youngest tree acquired a new branch. At its end hung a key made of silver bark. Its teeth matched no lock the traveler could remember.

## The Serpent Beneath the Sky

The orchard has no horizon. Its distant trees become smaller until they resemble scales. Beyond them, the violet sky bends downward and continues beneath the ground.

The woman says the sky is the inside of a folded wing. She says the trees grew where a serpent laid its name to rest. She also says that neither statement should be spoken while standing beside the well.

The traveler spoke both. The water became clear enough to show a long body coiled around an empty cradle. Its scales were silver, except for one dark place beneath its jaw. There, a name might once have been written.

The basket rose without a rope. Its blue feathers were wet. Inside lay a single pear, already cut, its interior holding a little field and a curtain moving in ordinary wind.

## The Bark's Narrow Sentence

The silver key carried marks along its stem:

*Ozhae — r — vyndul — ashun.*

The traveler pronounced only the space between them. The well answered with the sound of a door closing softly. Beneath the woman's stool, the returned door turned upright.

Its frame contained the cupboard from his house. The cupboard stood open. Inside were shoes too small for him, a folded coat, and an unbroken dark pear resting on a white plate.

He took nothing. The woman wrapped the key in her glove, and the glove's fingers became still. The youngest tree lowered its roots toward the ground for the first time.

Far below, the coiled creature shifted around the empty cradle. The dark place beneath its jaw caught a little light, but no letters could be seen.

## What Remains Outside

The traveler left through the curtain carrying an empty wooden frame. He did not remember lifting it. At home, he hung it where the door had stood. Through it he could see the ordinary room, with one difference: its cupboard was no longer there.

The field remains open to anyone who can find the curtain. The woman still brings her stool at noon. Her glove has acquired a narrow opening at the wrist, and sometimes she holds it up to the light as if checking whether something might pass through.

In the orchard, silver branches bend beneath dark fruit. The well reflects its unknown clouds. A young tree keeps one root suspended above the soil.

And when the violet sky grows quiet, something beneath it moves its tongue around a place where a name used to be.
